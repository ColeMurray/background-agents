# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Engineering teams in a trusted, single-tenant organization use Open-Inspect to start, supervise, and
collaborate with background coding agents from the web, Slack, GitHub, Linear, and automation
workflows. They need to understand what an agent is doing, intervene safely, and preserve repository
and identity boundaries without interrupting their own development work.

## Product Purpose

Open-Inspect provides durable, observable coding sessions backed by isolated development
environments. Success means authorized users can delegate work, follow progress, collaborate, and
recover from failures while session ownership, costs, credentials, approvals, and external effects
remain explicit and auditable.

## Brand Personality

Focused, trustworthy, and technically direct. The interface should feel like an engineering tool
that makes complex runtime state legible without turning agent behavior into spectacle.

## Anti-references

- Consumer chat interfaces that hide execution state behind a single typing indicator.
- Decorative AI dashboards with glowing gradients, glass panels, or anthropomorphic agents.
- Autopilot controls that blur queued work, active steering, cancellation, and destructive actions.
- Dense operations consoles that rely on color alone or require pointer precision.

## Design Principles

1. Make authority visible: permissions, ownership, and capability limits should be understandable at
   the point of action.
2. Distinguish intent: queued prompts, immediate follow-ups, steering, and lifecycle actions must
   never look interchangeable.
3. Reveal operational detail progressively: show status and the next useful action first, with task
   structure and diagnostics available without exposing hidden prompts or credentials.
4. Preserve flow: use familiar controls, stable layouts, concise copy, and bounded live updates.
5. Design for recovery: retries, indeterminate outcomes, cancellation, and archived states need
   clear explanations and safe next steps.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Every action must be keyboard-operable with visible focus, accessible names,
non-color status cues, polite live announcements, responsive touch targets, and layouts that work on
small screens. Respect reduced-motion preferences and avoid motion that is required to understand
state.
