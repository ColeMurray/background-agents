This page explains who can see and prompt a session, how to bring a colleague into one, and what each person's role lets them do.

## Ownership and visibility

A session has fixed ownership and changeable visibility. It is either workspace-owned or owned by a team; making a team-owned session **Workspace** visible does not transfer ownership or let non-members act in it. The session's owning user also matters for private access and sharing controls.

| Visibility | Who can read, with session read permission                                                                                                           |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Workspace  | Admitted workspace users, including Viewers.                                                                                                         |
| Team       | Members of the owning team, plus workspace Administrators and Owners, when Teams enforcement is on.                                                  |
| Private    | The owning user and explicitly added collaborators. Workspace Owners have audited break-glass read access; Administrators do not get that exception. |

<Callout type="warn" title="Check your deployment's enforcement mode">
  `TEAMS_ENFORCEMENT` defaults to `shadow`. In `shadow` or `off`, non-private Team visibility does
  not enforce read isolation from other workspace readers. Private access and team-owned non-read
  actions are enforced in every mode. Before relying on Team visibility to restrict readers, ask
  your operator to confirm `on` in [Deployment](/administration/deployment).
</Callout>

Participant labels record contributions; appearing in the timeline or Participants list does not grant access. Discovery filters such as **Started by me** are not access boundaries either. See [Teams](/administration/teams) for membership and [Inbox and search](/sessions/inbox-and-search) for discovery scopes.

Creating a session also requires permission to use its repository or environment, so a person may be able to open an existing session without being able to start a new one on the same repository.

## Sharing a session

Use **Copy link** (in the More menu above the composer on desktop, or in the "Session actions" menu on phones) to copy the session URL. Sending the link does not grant access: the recipient must already pass the session's access checks.

For a private session, an authorized owning user or workspace Owner can open **Collaborators** in the right sidebar, choose **Add collaborator**, and click **Add**. Use **Remove** to revoke a grant. Team-owned sessions accept only current members of the owning team as collaborators. If a collaborator leaves that team, their stored grant no longer gives access. Managing collaborators on a team-owned session also requires current membership, even for the owning user or workspace Owner.

Collaborators still need the appropriate workspace permissions to prompt or use sandbox tools. A workspace Owner reading through audited break-glass access does not thereby gain collaboration or sandbox access: those require being the owning user or a collaborator. Other administrative actions remain subject to their own checks.

## Change visibility

1. Open the session's right sidebar, or **Details** on a small screen.
2. If you are authorized to change visibility, choose **Workspace**, **Team**, or **Private** under **Visibility**. Team requires an owning team; Private requires an owning user.
3. Review **Include child sessions**, which is checked by default, then click **Save**.

Including children when changing to Workspace or Team shows a confirmation: **private child sessions will also change to that visibility**. Clear the checkbox to change only this session. You must be authorized for every included descendant; when offered after a failure, **Retry without child sessions** applies the change only to the parent.

For non-private sessions, the owning user, owning team's leads, and workspace administrators can change visibility if they meet the access checks. For private sessions, that authority is limited to the owning user or workspace Owner. Team-owned changes always require current owning-team membership. Visibility changes never change ownership.

## Presence

The **Participants** section at the top of the right sidebar shows who is in the session: an avatar for each participant (up to four) and a count such as "2 prompt engineers". A green dot on an avatar means that person is connected right now. Presence updates live as people open and close the page.

Everyone connected receives the same event stream, so a prompt sent by one person and the agent's response to it appear for all participants at the same time.

## Prompt attribution

Each prompt in the timeline is labeled with its author: "You" for your own prompts, and the other person's name and avatar for theirs. The attribution is carried all the way into git. Commits the agent makes while working on a prompt use that prompt author's SCM name and email as the commit author, so a branch that two people steered has both of them in its history. Pull requests are opened with the prompting user's GitHub identity when one is connected. See [reviewing changes](/sessions/reviewing-changes) for how attribution and commit signing interact.

Prompts queued by PR Feedback Autofix carry a "Resumed by PR feedback" header that names the source (a review or a PR comment, from a human or a bot).

## Collaboration patterns

- **Start and steer.** One person writes the initial prompt and moves on. A colleague opens the link later, reads the timeline, and sends the follow-up that corrects course. Follow-ups queue behind the running turn regardless of who sent them.
- **Pair debugging.** Two people watch the same run. One reads tool output in the timeline while the other checks the diff in the Changes panel, and either can stop the turn if it goes wrong.
- **Hand-off.** Share the link at the end of your day. The next person sees the full history, the queued prompts, and the current sandbox state, and continues in the same branch.
- **Teaching.** Newer team members can read sessions they have access to and see how others prompt and how the agent works through a task.

Because follow-ups are queued in order, agree on who is steering before both of you send corrections at once. The queued prompt stack above the composer shows what is waiting, and anyone with lifecycle access can remove a queued prompt.

## What not to put in a session

People authorized to read a session, including eligible Viewers, can see its recorded content:

- The text of every prompt and every attached image.
- The agent's responses, tool calls, and command output, including anything printed by a script.
- Screenshots and recordings.
- The diff and the branch.

Choose visibility before adding sensitive content, and remember that it can be widened later, including through a parent session's visibility change. Do not ask the agent to print secrets in the sandbox. Keep credentials in [Secrets](/configure/secrets) rather than prompts; a command that echoes an environment variable puts its value in the timeline.

## Role effects inside a session

Workspace roles grant feature permissions, not unconditional access to every session. The table describes built-in permissions after the session's visibility and membership checks pass.

| Role                    | In a session                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Viewer                  | Read only. Can open the session, watch the timeline, and read the diff and media. Cannot prompt, stop, rename, archive, or use sandbox tools.                                                                                     |
| Member                  | Can prompt and contribute, and can stop turns, remove queued prompts, rename, archive and unarchive (`sessions.lifecycle`), retry a failed diff, and use the terminal, code-server, VNC, and tunnels (`sessions.sandbox_access`). |
| Administrator and Owner | Member feature permissions plus workspace administration, still subject to session access checks.                                                                                                                                 |

Lifecycle actions (stop, rename, archive, unarchive, remove a queued prompt, retry the diff) need `sessions.lifecycle`. Prompting needs `sessions.collaborate`. Sandbox tools need `sessions.sandbox_access`. **Every non-read action on a team-owned session requires current owning-team membership**, including for its owning user, Administrators, and Owners. Private prompting and sandbox access additionally require being the owning user or a collaborator.

Role changes and suspensions take effect on the next request. A browser that already has the session open is rechecked at least every five minutes, so someone whose access was removed can keep a live connection for up to five minutes.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom                                                                                   | Cause                                                                                                                                                 | Fix                                                                                                              |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The Sessions page says "You do not have permission to view sessions."                     | Your account holds no role that can read sessions. Every built-in role, Viewer included, can, so your workspace access has been removed or suspended. | Ask a workspace Owner or Administrator to check your account under Settings › Workspace access.                  |
| A person whose access was removed or suspended still sees live updates in an open session | Live connections are rechecked at least every five minutes, not on every event.                                                                       | Wait up to five minutes; the connection closes on the next recheck. New requests are refused immediately.        |
| A session link is unavailable, or you can read but cannot act                             | Visibility, collaborator status, team membership, or feature permissions do not allow the operation                                                   | Ask the owning user or team lead to check access. Administrator status alone does not bypass team action checks. |
